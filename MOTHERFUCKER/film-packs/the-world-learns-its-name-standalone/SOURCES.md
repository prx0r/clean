# Research basis and limits

This is a comparative artwork. It puts perceptual science, philosophy of
language, and Trika thought into aesthetic tension; it does not claim they are
the same theory or that one proves another.

## Constructive speech perception

- Richard M. Warren, “Perceptual Restoration of Missing Speech Sounds”:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5409744/
- Harry McGurk and John MacDonald, “Hearing lips and seeing voices”:
  https://www.nature.com/articles/264746a0
- Ediz Sohoglu et al., “Predictive Top-Down Integration of Prior Knowledge
  during Speech Perception”:
  https://www.jneurosci.org/content/32/25/8443

What is used: listeners can perceptually restore absent speech material;
visible articulation can alter what phoneme is heard; prior knowledge can
shape the processing of degraded speech.

What is not claimed: that sensory evidence is irrelevant, that context can make
any interpretation true, or that these effects establish a metaphysics of
language.

## Inner speech

- Charles Fernyhough and Ben Alderson-Day, “Inner Speech: Development,
  Cognitive Functions, Phenomenology, and Neurobiology”:
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/

What is used: inner speech is heterogeneous and can be condensed, dialogic, or
expanded rather than being a single silent playback mechanism.

What is not claimed: that the film's spiral is a literal neural model or that
inner speech has one universal visual form.

## Bhartṛhari and sentence meaning

- Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Bhartṛhari”:
  https://iep.utm.edu/bhartrihari/
- Marco Ferrante, *Indian Perspectives on Consciousness, Language and Self*
  (Routledge, 2021), repository research text:
  `resources/pdfs/full-books/ferrante-indian-perspectives.txt`

What is used: Bhartṛhari treats language and cognition as deeply entangled;
sentence meaning is grasped as an integrated whole rather than mechanically
assembled from independent containers; `pratibhā` names the flash-like
comprehension of that whole.

The narration includes one public-domain verse, *Vākyapadīya* 1.131, with a new
translation made for this composition.

What is not claimed: that `pratibhā` is a brain event, a MIDI cadence, or a
scientific synonym for predictive integration.

## Trika, recognition, and levels of speech

- Jaideva Singh's edition of Abhinavagupta's
  *Parā-trīśikā-vivaraṇa*, public archive:
  https://archive.org/details/abhinavagupta-para-trishika-vivarana-the-secret-of-tantric-mysticism-jaideva-singh
- Repository research on Utpaladeva:
  `resources/by-scholar/utpaladeva/utpaladeva-philosopher-recognition.txt`
- Ferrante, repository text listed above.

What is used: Trika gives the film a philosophical account of recognition,
reflexive awareness, and the progressive manifestation of speech. The artistic
constraint index uses conventional Earth=`1`, Shiva=`36` tattva numbering.

Important correction: Bhartṛhari describes `vaikharī`, `madhyamā`, and
`paśyantī`; later Trika sources articulate `parā` as a fourth level.
Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta drew deeply on Bhartṛhari while rejecting
`sphoṭa`. The film preserves that disagreement instead of presenting one
seamless historical doctrine.

What is not claimed: that the four levels are four brain regions, processing
stages, frequency bands, colors, or MIDI registers. The Pratyabhijñā account is
philosophical and metaphysical; it does not follow empirically from the speech
studies above.

## Rasa and shader practice

- LYGIA:
  https://github.com/patriciogonzalezvivo/lygia
- The Book of Shaders:
  https://thebookofshaders.com/

Rasa governs relationships among pacing, register, movement, color, and
expectation. It is not converted into a coordinate or mood meter. The shader
uses the repository's original procedural libraries and follows LYGIA's
compositional spirit; it does not copy gallery shaders.
