Physics can describe fields, symmetries, and dynamics. But description leaves one question untouched. Why is any process manifest at all? Why is there felt presence, rather than events merely occurring without appearance?

We must separate two questions. How does a physical system change? And why is change present to itself, or present at all? Quantum field theory addresses the first with extraordinary precision. It does not, by itself, answer the second.

Abhinavagupta's answer has three terms. Prakasha: manifestation or luminous presence. Vimarsha: self-articulation, the capacity of presence to register and determine itself. Spanda: the dynamic pulsation through which implicit unity becomes explicit difference without ceasing to be one reality.

The argument can be stated formally. Experience entails manifestation. Abstract structure alone does not logically entail manifestation. Another unmanifest state cannot supply it without repeating the problem. Yet bare manifestation cannot explain a determinate world. Determination requires differentiation, and differentiation requires dynamism. Therefore the proposed ground is not inert awareness, but self-manifesting pulsation.

A serious objection remains. Perhaps organization is enough. Perhaps sufficiently complex information processing simply is presence. But this explains functional discrimination and report. It does not yet derive why those functions are manifest rather than dark. The bridge is asserted, not demonstrated.

This is why spanda should not be equated with quantum fluctuation. A quantum fluctuation is change within a mathematically specified physical field. Spanda names the claimed self-manifesting differentiation of reality as such. The structures may be compared artistically, but one does not prove the other.

Three broad positions remain. Physical structuralism says organization yields presence, but owes the bridge. Russellian monism gives matter an intrinsic nature, but still owes unified self-apprehension. Abhinavagupta begins with manifestation, self-articulation, and dynamism as inseparable. It is the most complete architecture here, but it is still a metaphysical proposal, not an empirical result.

The epistemic ledger must remain explicit. Physics supports dynamical structure. Consciousness science studies correlations, access, report, and integration. Trika claims that manifestation is fundamental. The bridge is a structural comparison: dynamic differentiation within an already manifest field. The non-claim is that modern physics has verified spanda.

So why does reality appear? The strongest Trika answer is that appearing is not an added property of an otherwise hidden world. Reality is manifestation capable of articulating itself, and its multiplicity is the activity of that self-articulation. The unresolved frontier is whether this is the necessary ground of experience, or a profound interpretation of a fact science has not yet explained.
