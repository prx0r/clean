## The tonal-center issue

Do not insert D into every chord. That would likely turn the tonal center into a drone and weaken harmonic motion. Tonal persistence should instead measure:

* duration-weighted pitch-class hierarchy;
* D emphasis at formal boundaries;
* recurring cadential or ground-bass returns;
* motif anchoring;
* sliding-window correlation with the intended mode.

Likewise, "zero common-tone violations" only measures chord-set overlap. Actual voice-leading validation needs registered voices, displacement, crossings, independence, and continuity of voice identity.

## The central conceptual repair

I would change the question to:

> How can a pattern retain identity when none of its material carriers persist?

And the thesis to:

> Identity can persist through carrier turnover when relational organization is repeatedly transferred, renewed, and remembered.

That is compatible with dissipative structures: organized patterns can persist in open, nonequilibrium systems through continuing throughput rather than through static matter. Entropy must be stated relative to a defined system; an open subsystem can become more ordered while total entropy increases. [MIT statistical physics notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-8-010-introduction-to-statistical-physics-summer-2018/mitres_8_010su18_lec5.pdf), [Prigogine's Nobel lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/prigogine-lecture.pdf), [APS review of pattern formation](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/RevModPhys.65.851)

That means the epistemic claims should become:

* "Entropy is absolute" → reject.
* "Vibration precedes form" → metaphysical proposition, not established by QFT.
* "Persistent organization can survive material turnover" → conditionally supported for open systems with energy and information transfer.

## The right musical form

Use a **migrating passacaglia**.

A conventional passacaglia keeps one ground bass, which would secretly become the permanent substrate. Instead:

* preserve the ground's interval-and-rhythm relation;
* transfer it between cello, viola, winds, percussion, and harmonic texture;
* occasionally remove every original pitch while retaining its transformation;
* let accumulated transformations alter later repetitions;
* reveal identity as invariance across changing carriers.

That is stronger structural homology than merely naming "passacaglia."

## Most importantly, implement memory

Add a history-dependent state, not just another hand-authored waypoint:

```
\dot m(t)=\frac{d(t)-m(t)}{\tau}
```

Here (d(t)) is the current pattern-driving signal and (m(t)) is its decaying trace. Feed that trace into:

* GLSL topology and afterimages;
* motif survival and orchestration transfer;
* narration timing;
* the final state.

The same six-dimensional state should therefore render differently depending on how it was reached. That is the actual artistic proof of memory.

I would also replace the current axes with behavioral mechanisms:

1. `carrier_turnover`
2. `trace_retention`
3. `phase_coherence`
4. `energy_throughput`
5. `transfer_fidelity`
6. `recognition_invariance`

The ending should not collapse. Let carrier visibility and throughput approach zero while trace retention and recognition remain nonzero—perhaps a barely visible relational thread surviving after its material embodiment has vanished.

So my recommendation is: don't begin the expensive full visual attempt yet. First repair the question, axes, epistemic claims, migrating-passacaglia architecture, and causal memory mechanism. Then produce a 30–60 second prototype spanning emergence, transfer, silence, and return. If that communicates persistence without narration, the composition is ready to scale.
