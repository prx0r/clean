# Satsang Reputation — The Research Contribution Engine

## Core Loop

User asks a question → question enters truth map → if novel and underdetermined → triggers research pipeline → content gets created → user who asked gets credit → content performance feeds back → user reputation grows → reputation unlocks research platform features.

## The Contribution Cycle

### 1. Question Asked

A user asks anything on Satsang. Could be a search bar query, a dating profile icebreaker, a comment on a video, a direct question to the platform.

The system normalizes it to a truth map question slug. If the question already exists, the user gets linked to existing content. If it's novel and has genuine tension, it enters the pipeline.

### 2. Research Pipeline Triggered

The new truth map entry gets picked up by the hypothesis engine. Relevant ROs are gathered. An EO is created. The writing factory produces an essay. The video factory may produce a video. The user who asked gets notified at each stage.

### 3. Contribution Credited

When content publishes, the original question-asker gets:

- **Q-score:** +1 for each question that triggered content
- **Engagement share:** a portion of attention budget ad revenue from that content goes to the question-asker (small but meaningful — their question literally earned money)
- **Attribution:** their name/handle appears as "question by" on the content

### 4. Content Performance Feeds Back

If the content performs well (high retention, high completion, high engagement), the question-asker's reputation increases further. The system learns: this user asks good questions — questions that lead to content people actually want to watch.

The reputation becomes a signal: questions from high-reputation users get prioritized by the hypothesis engine.

### 5. Reputation Unlock

| Reputation Tier | Q-score Required | Unlocks |
|----------------|------------------|---------|
| Curious | 0 | Ask questions, get credited |
| Contributor | 10 | Questions prioritized in hypothesis engine |
| Researcher | 50 | Can submit source material for review |
| Scholar | 200 | Can submit alternative translations to TOs |
| Guide | 500 | Can vote on truth map confidence levels |
| Sage | 2000 | Can propose edits to EOs |

## The Collective Research Engine

Over time, the sum of all user questions shapes what the platform produces. The hypothesis engine doesn't just scan the truth map for underdetermined questions — it ranks them by:

- **Novelty:** hasn't been asked before
- **Depth:** genuine tension, not trivial
- **User interest:** how many people are watching/following this question
- **Questioner reputation:** high-reputation users' questions get priority
- **Production feasibility:** do the ROs exist to support an EO?

This means the community literally directs the research. The platform becomes a collective intelligence engine where the users' curiosity drives what gets investigated, and the factories turn that curiosity into polished content.

## The Flywheel

```
More users → more questions → more truth map entries
  → more content produced → more engagement
    → more ad revenue → more attention budget
      → more earned by question-askers → more incentive to ask good questions
        → better questions → better content → more users
```

The platform stops being a content library and becomes a **research organism** where every user's genuine curiosity feeds the whole system. The boundary between consumer and producer dissolves — everyone who asks a good question is participating in the research programme.
