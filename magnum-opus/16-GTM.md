# Satsang.digital — Go-to-Market Strategy

## The Core Insight

You don't have a cold start problem. You have a content library, a factory pipeline, and a unique translation engine that produces material no one else has. The platform is a value-add around what already exists, not a gamble on what might come.

---

## Phase 0: The Obvious Start (Now)

Upload all existing video essays to satsang.digital. Cross-post to YouTube. The platform already has a full content library on day one.

At this stage, the platform looks like: "here's our research, beautifully hosted, with full provenance back to source texts." Not a social platform yet — a really good content site with a unique value prop (every claim links to an RO, which links to a source text, which has a translation you can inspect).

**Key action:** Every video, every essay, every RO gets a home on satsang.digital before anywhere else.

---

## Phase 1: Drop the TO Bomb (3-6 months)

When the Sanskrit translation pipeline has 10+ significant texts (Spanda, Vijnanabhairava, Tantraloka selected chapters, IPK), publish the TO browser.

**The news moment:** No one else has verified, versioned, multi-pass DeepSeek Sanskrit translations with full provenance. The philology world notices.

**First 100 users:** Genuine scholars who request accounts to comment, suggest alternatives, debate translations. They're not there for the platform — they're there for the TOs.

**Key action:** Make the TO browser beautiful, functional, and open. Let scholars see the work without requiring login. The account gate goes up only when they want to contribute.

---

## Phase 2: The Legitimacy Flywheel (6-12 months)

Those 100 scholars give the platform credibility. Other foundations see: "Vimarsha Foundation has a channel here, Sanskritree runs here, these scholars are discussing translations." It becomes a legitimate place, not a startup.

**Invite:**
- **Foundations** — verified channels, live streams of teachings, retreats, events. Free for them. They get the attention budget system when it's ready.
- **Spiritual teachers** — a better home than YouTube where their content gets demonetized or algorithmically buried.
- **Educational creators** — philosophy/spirituality/documentary niche already aligned.

**The pitch:** "Your audience is already here. Our users are your audience — people who study Sanskrit, read philosophy, meditate. 80% revenue split. No algorithm manipulation. Full data ownership."

**Key action:** Personally invite 20-50 foundations, teachers, and creators. Help them set up their channels. Treat them as partners, not users.

---

## Phase 3: Community Activates (12-24 months)

**At 1,000 users:**
- Philosophy pages (every user's "My Philosophy" manifesto)
- Feed builder (topic selection, creator prioritization)
- Scholar contribution system (comments on verses, alternative translations)

**At 10,000 users:**
- Birth chart profiles
- Dating/philosophy matching
- Attention budget

**At 100,000 users:**
- Curated ads (ethical brands only)
- Live streaming (study groups, satsangs, events)

---

## The Golden Rule

**Don't build social features until users ask for them.**

The most dangerous thing is building dating before anyone has a profile. Natural progression:

```
TO browser → scholars join → they ask for comments → build comments
→ they ask for profiles → build profiles → they ask to connect → build messaging
→ they ask for feeds → build feed builder → they ask for discovery → build matching
```

The platform grows from actual demand, not imagined features.

---

## Unit Economics Reality

- Hosting: Cloudflare Stream ~$5/TB (1,000 users watching 1hr/day = ~$150/mo)
- Storage: R2 ~$0.015/GB/mo
- LLM: DeepSeek API ~$20/mo
- Total monthly burn for 1,000 users: ~$200
- Revenue needed: $0.20/user/mo

This doesn't need to be a business. It needs to be a service. One sponsored research grant covers it.
