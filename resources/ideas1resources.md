# Resources for the Quantum Tantra Project

## The right corpus

It should not be limited to papers that explicitly mention Tantra—there are very few. The useful library has four layers:

1. **direct Abhinavagupta–science comparisons**;
2. **phenomenology translated into computational/neuroscientific models**;
3. **research on minimal, nondual and selfless consciousness**;
4. **metaphysics of information, possibility, causation and fundamental consciousness**.

Biernacki's two papers are still the clearest direct precedents. Her method is exactly what you want: isolate a precise problem, reconstruct Abhinavagupta without flattening him, introduce a modern theory in its own terms, compare their explanatory structures, and let each expose a limitation in the other.

# I. Direct Abhinavagupta, Trika and cognitive-science bridges

## 1. Loriliai Biernacki — "A Cognitive Science View of Abhinavagupta's Understanding of Consciousness" (2014)

The essential starting point. It compares Abhinavagupta with contemporary philosophy of consciousness and asks whether subjectivity can be given explanatory priority without abandoning material embodiment.

- DOI: [10.3390/rel5030767](https://doi.org/10.3390/rel5030767)
- Full text: [https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/5/3/767](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/5/3/767)

## 2. Loriliai Biernacki — "Connecting Consciousness to Physical Causality: Abhinavagupta's Phenomenology of Subjectivity and Tononi's Integrated Information Theory" (2016)

The IIT 3.0 paper. It argues that both Abhinavagupta and IIT begin with phenomenology and connect intrinsic experience with causal efficacy.

- DOI: [10.3390/rel7070087](https://doi.org/10.3390/rel7070087)
- Full text: [https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/7/7/87](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/7/7/87)

## 3. Loriliai Biernacki — "Imagining the Body in Tantric Contemplative Practice" (2017)

Especially useful for essays on *bhāvanā*, visualisation, embodiment and whether imagination creates rather than merely represents experiential realities.

- DOI: [10.1186/s40613-016-0043-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40613-016-0043-7)

# The five best structural exemplars

These are not simply the five most informative papers. They are the five to give an essay-writing agent and say: **Produce work shaped like this.**

## Exemplar 1: Biernacki — Abhinavagupta and IIT

[https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/7/7/87](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/7/7/87)

### Why imitate it

It begins with **one shared problem**—how experience can possess physical causal efficacy. It does not compare two entire worldviews indiscriminately.

### Weakness to improve

It is sometimes too generous about the convergence. Your version should include a dedicated **failure-of-equivalence section** and ask what evidence would discriminate the positions.

## Exemplar 2: Albarracin et al. — Husserl and active inference

[https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09058)

### Why imitate it

This is the best model for formal comparative translation. Its underlying structure is: phenomenological distinction → formal counterpart → qualified mapping. It identifies which elements can be represented using generative models and which cannot.

### Apply it to Trika

icchā → prior preference; jñāna → generative modelling; kriyā → policy selection. Then immediately ask where the mapping breaks.

## Exemplar 3: Metzinger — Minimal Phenomenal Experience

[https://www.philosophie.fb05.uni-mainz.de/files/2020/03/Metzinger_MPE1_PMS_2020.pdf](https://www.philosophie.fb05.uni-mainz.de/files/2020/03/Metzinger_MPE1_PMS_2020.pdf)

### Why imitate it

Metzinger begins with reports that sound metaphysically explosive but first treats them as a **phenomenological target**, not proof of a doctrine. The method: isolate a reproducible experiential phenomenon; strip away unnecessary conceptual commitments; identify minimal phenomenal features; construct a model; remain agnostic about metaphysical interpretations.

## Exemplar 4: Laukkonen and Slagter — Predictive processing and meditation

[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.06.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.06.021)

Preprint: [https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/5sw6m](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/5sw6m)

### Why imitate it

It turns a heterogeneous spiritual practice into a coherent, falsifiable model by identifying a unifying mechanism. Its architecture is especially suitable for videos: present the apparent chaos of practices; introduce one explanatory framework; order practices along a principled continuum; derive predictions.

## Exemplar 5: Varela — Neurophenomenology

### Why imitate it

Varela refuses both extremes: reducing experience to neural measurements, and treating introspection as infallible revelation. First-person and third-person evidence constrain each other. This gives the whole project its methodological constitution.

# The house structure your papers should use

A strong paper should follow this sequence:

## 1. Exact question

Not "What does Tantra say about consciousness?" But "Is *icchā–jñāna–kriyā* a logically necessary architecture of manifestation, or a theological projection of human agency?"

## 2. Contemporary scientific target

Explain one theory accurately enough that a specialist would recognise it: active inference, IIT, self-model theory, constructor theory, information closure, predictive processing.

## 3. Historical reconstruction

Use primary Sanskrit passages and strong scholarship to establish what Abhinavagupta actually claims.

## 4. Comparative invariant

Identify the exact structure shared by both systems: value → model → action, or intrinsic experience ↔ intrinsic causality.

## 5. Non-equivalence

State precisely why the systems remain different: organismic versus cosmic, epistemic versus ontological, descriptive versus causal, computational versus experiential, constrained possibility versus absolute power.

## 6. Mutual pressure test

What each system exposes in the other. Trika asks active inference where value and manifestness ultimately originate. Active inference asks Trika for a mechanism of contraction and individuation.

## 7. Best current answer

What the evidence and arguments currently favour.

## 8. Open problem

The remaining explanatory gap.

## 9. Research consequences

Possible empirical, textual or formal tests.
