# 100 Sources — Ranked by Usefulness for the Tantrāloka–Consciousness-Science Pipeline

**Priority ordering:**
1. direct Trika/Pratyabhijñā ↔ cognition/science;
2. Pratyabhijñā ↔ Buddhism;
3. Buddhism ↔ philosophy of mind/cognitive science;
4. contemplative neuroscience supplying testable mechanisms.

"Paper" includes several decisive book chapters and open preprints. Academia links sometimes require a free login; DOI links may lead to abstracts if the publisher copy is paywalled.

---

# I. Trika, Tantra and cognitive science

## Direct bridges

1. **Sthaneshwar Timalsina — "A Cognitive Approach to Tantric Language"**
   Uses metaphor, metonymy and conceptual blending to explain how tantric language reorganizes cognition rather than merely concealing doctrine. One of the closest direct Tantra–cognitive-science papers available.
   [https://www.academia.edu/30159814/A_Cognitive_Approach_to_Tantric_Language](https://www.academia.edu/30159814/A_Cognitive_Approach_to_Tantric_Language)

2. **Timalsina — "Introduction to Cognitive Science and the Study of Yoga and Tantra"**
   Sets out the interdisciplinary programme: tantric practices as structured transformations of body schema, imagination, affect and cognition.
   [https://www.academia.edu/36221429/Introduction_to_Cognitive_Science_and_the_Study_of_Yoga_and_Tantra](https://www.academia.edu/36221429/Introduction_to_Cognitive_Science_and_the_Study_of_Yoga_and_Tantra)

3. **Timalsina — "Reconstructing the Tantric Body"**
   Shows visualization dismantling habitual embodiment and constructing a transformed body. Excellent for comparison with predictive body models and active inference.
   [https://www.academia.edu/3626612/Reconstructing_the_Tantric_Body_Elements_of_the_Symbolism_of_Body_in_the_Monistic_Kaula_and_Trika_Tantric_Traditions](https://www.academia.edu/3626612/Reconstructing_the_Tantric_Body_Elements_of_the_Symbolism_of_Body_in_the_Monistic_Kaula_and_Trika_Tantric_Traditions)

4. **Timalsina — "Visualization in Hindu Practice"**
   Treats visualization as embodied world-making, not private fantasy. Use beside mental-imagery neuroscience and generative models.
   [https://www.academia.edu/34697954/Visualization_in_Hindu_Practice](https://www.academia.edu/34697954/Visualization_in_Hindu_Practice)

5. **Timalsina — "Meditation and Imagination"**
   Examines imagination as an instrument for creating a new ritual and perceptual world. The key scientific question is whether bhāvanā modifies only experience or the agent's whole policy-and-affordance space.
   [https://www.academia.edu/43640836/Meditation_and_imagination](https://www.academia.edu/43640836/Meditation_and_imagination)

6. **Jeffrey Lidke — "The Resounding Field of Visualised Self-Awareness"**
   Interprets Śrīyantra ritual through synaesthesia, multisensory integration and hyperconnectivity. Ambitious and occasionally speculative, but exactly within your niche.
   [https://www.academia.edu/13709425/The_Resounding_Field_of_Visualised_Self_Awareness_The_Generation_of_Synesthetic_Consciousness_in_the_Sri_Yantra_Ritual_of_Nityasodasikarnava_Tantra](https://www.academia.edu/13709425/The_Resounding_Field_of_Visualised_Self_Awareness_The_Generation_of_Synesthetic_Consciousness_in_the_Sri_Yantra_Ritual_of_Nityasodasikarnava_Tantra)

7. **Glen Alexander Hayes — "Possible Selves, Body Schemas, and Sādhana"**
   Uses cognitive science and neuroscience to interpret tantric body construction. Useful for treating deity yoga as controlled revision of body schema and identity.
   [https://doi.org/10.3390/rel5030684](https://doi.org/10.3390/rel5030684)

8. **Glen Hayes — "The Yoga of the Cosmic Puruṣa"**
   Explores tantric body mapping and its relation to cosmology, embodiment and identity. Useful historical material for comparing the subtle body with hierarchical self-models.
   [https://www.academia.edu/216464/The_Yoga_of_the_Cosmic_Purusa](https://www.academia.edu/216464/The_Yoga_of_the_Cosmic_Purusa)

9. **Timalsina — "The Body of Time"**
   Relevant to Tantrāloka chapter 6: embodiment, temporality and ritual compression of cosmic time. Pair with temporal binding and predictive-processing accounts of experienced time.
   [https://sbsuny.academia.edu/SthaneshwarTimalsina](https://sbsuny.academia.edu/SthaneshwarTimalsina)

10. **Timalsina — "Tantric Visual Culture and the Cognitive Science of Religion"**
    Treats images as cognitive operators embedded in practice rather than passive symbols.
    [https://sbsuny.academia.edu/SthaneshwarTimalsina](https://sbsuny.academia.edu/SthaneshwarTimalsina)

## Pratyabhijñā's philosophy of consciousness

11. **Isabelle Ratié — "Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta on the Freedom of Consciousness"** ★
    One of the foundational readings. Consciousness is not a static witness but active freedom disclosed in ordinary cognition; compare this directly with agency, counterfactual depth and active inference.
    [https://www.academia.edu/25985292/Utpaladeva_and_Abhinavagupta_on_the_Freedom_of_Consciousness](https://www.academia.edu/25985292/Utpaladeva_and_Abhinavagupta_on_the_Freedom_of_Consciousness)

12. **Ratié — "The Dreamer and the Yogin"** ★
    Shows how Pratyabhijñā appropriates Buddhist idealist arguments while rejecting their metaphysical consequences. Essential for constructing the Trika → Buddhism → cognitive-science route.
    [https://www.academia.edu/12663114/The_Dreamer_and_the_Yogin_On_the_relationship_between_Buddhist_and_Saiva_idealisms](https://www.academia.edu/12663114/The_Dreamer_and_the_Yogin_On_the_relationship_between_Buddhist_and_Saiva_idealisms)

13. **Ratié — "Can One Prove That Something Exists Beyond Consciousness?"** ★
    Reconstructs the Śaiva criticism of the Sautrāntika inference to external objects. Place beside indirect realism, representationalism and controlled-hallucination theories.
    [https://www.academia.edu/12663122/Can_one_prove_that_something_exists_beyond_consciousness_A_Saiva_criticism_of_the_Sautrantika_inference_of_external_objects](https://www.academia.edu/12663122/Can_one_prove_that_something_exists_beyond_consciousness_A_Saiva_criticism_of_the_Sautrantika_inference_of_external_objects)

14. **Ratié — "How Free Is Imagination According to Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta?"** ★
    Asks whether imagination is autonomous creation or constrained recombination. Perfect for generative models, counterfactual processing and mental imagery.
    [https://www.academia.edu/12663100/How_free_is_imagination_according_to_Utpaladeva_and_Abhinavagupta](https://www.academia.edu/12663100/How_free_is_imagination_according_to_Utpaladeva_and_Abhinavagupta)

15. **Ratié — "Otherness in the Pratyabhijñā Philosophy"**
    Explains how nondual idealism preserves experienced otherness without locating it outside consciousness. Relevant to differentiation, alterity and Markov-blanket-style individuation.
    [https://www.academia.edu/12663014/Otherness_in_the_Pratyabhijna_Philosophy](https://www.academia.edu/12663014/Otherness_in_the_Pratyabhijna_Philosophy)

16. **Ratié — "Some Hitherto Unknown Fragments of Utpaladeva's Vivṛti I: Other Conscious Streams"** ★
    Directly addresses the other-minds problem and Buddhist arguments about multiple streams. One of your strongest possible comparative essays.
    [https://www.academia.edu/19863679/Some_hitherto_unknown_fragments_of_Utpaladeva_s_Viv%E1%B9%9Bti_I_on_the_Buddhist_controversy_over_the_existence_of_other_conscious_streams](https://www.academia.edu/19863679/Some_hitherto_unknown_fragments_of_Utpaladeva_s_Viv%E1%B9%9Bti_I_on_the_Buddhist_controversy_over_the_existence_of_other_conscious_streams)

17. **Ratié — "Some Hitherto Unknown Fragments of Utpaladeva's Vivṛti II: Against External Objects"**
    Primary material for Pratyabhijñā idealism and its rejection of mind-independent objects.
    [https://www.academia.edu/44231081/Some_Hitherto_Unknown_Fragments_of_Utpaladevas_Viv%E1%B9%9Bti_II_Against_the_Existence_of_External_Objects](https://www.academia.edu/44231081/Some_Hitherto_Unknown_Fragments_of_Utpaladevas_Viv%E1%B9%9Bti_II_Against_the_Existence_of_External_Objects)

18. **Ratié — "Studies in Utpaladeva's Vivṛti II: What Is Memory?"** ★
    Utpaladeva argues that memory requires continuity not captured by momentary cognitions. Compare with episodic memory, diachronic binding and self-model continuity.
    [https://www.academia.edu/4476016/Studies_in_Utpaladeva_s_%C4%AA%C5%9Bvarapratyabhij%C3%B1%C4%81_viv%E1%B9%9Bti_Part_II_What_is_memory](https://www.academia.edu/4476016/Studies_in_Utpaladeva_s_%C4%AA%C5%9Bvarapratyabhij%C3%B1%C4%81_viv%E1%B9%9Bti_Part_II_What_is_memory)

19. **Ratié — "Studies in Utpaladeva's Vivṛti III: Can a Cognition Become the Object of Another Cognition?"** ★
    Directly relevant to higher-order theories versus intrinsic reflexivity. Does consciousness require a second cognition to know the first?
    [https://www.academia.edu/4476018/Studies_in_Utpaladeva_s_%C4%AA%C5%9Bvarapratyabhij%C3%B1%C4%81_viv%E1%B9%9Bti_Part_III_Can_a_cognition_become_the_object_of_another_cognition](https://www.academia.edu/4476018/Studies_in_Utpaladeva_s_%C4%AA%C5%9Bvarapratyabhij%C3%B1%C4%81_viv%E1%B9%9Bti_Part_III_Can_a_cognition_become_the_object_of_another_cognition)

20. **Ratié — "Studies in Utpaladeva's Vivṛti I: Apoha and Anupalabdhi in a Śaiva Garb"**
    Shows Pratyabhijñā absorbing Buddhist exclusion semantics and theories of non-perception. Useful for concept formation and negative inference.
    [https://www.academia.edu/4476017/Studies_in_Utpaladeva_s_%C4%AA%C5%9Bvarapratyabhij%C3%B1%C4%81_viv%E1%B9%9Bti_Part_I_Apoha_and_anupalabdhi_in_a_%C5%9Aaiva_garb](https://www.academia.edu/4476017/Studies_in_Utpaladeva_s_%C4%AA%C5%9Bvarapratyabhij%C3%B1%C4%81_viv%E1%B9%9Bti_Part_I_Apoha_and_anupalabdhi_in_a_%C5%9Aaiva_garb)

21. **Ratié — "A Note on Śaṅkaranandana's Intuition According to Abhinavagupta"**
    Explores a Buddhist theory reinterpreted through Śaiva freedom and ultimate consciousness.
    [https://www.academia.edu/44673394/A_Note_on_Sankaranandanas_intuition_According_to_Abhinavagupta](https://www.academia.edu/44673394/A_Note_on_Sankaranandanas_intuition_According_to_Abhinavagupta)

22. **Raffaele Torella — "Utpaladeva's Lost Vivṛti on the Īśvarapratyabhijñā-kārikā"**
    Introduces recovered material on cognition, memory, reflexivity, yogic perception and the shared luminosity of cognition and object.
    [https://www.academia.edu/6417695/Utpaladevas_lost_Viv%E1%B9%9Bti_on_the_%C4%AA%C5%9Bvarapratyabhij%C3%B1a_k%C4%81rik%C4%81](https://www.academia.edu/6417695/Utpaladevas_lost_Viv%E1%B9%9Bti_on_the_%C4%AA%C5%9Bvarapratyabhij%C3%B1a_k%C4%81rik%C4%81)

23. **Torella — "In Search of Utpaladeva's Lost Vivṛti"**
    Includes important material on phenomena as reflections whose ultimate reality is consciousness.
    [https://www.academia.edu/32230777/In_search_of_Utpaladeva_s_lost_Vivrti_on_the_Pratyabhijna_treatise_a_report_on_the_latest_discoveries_with_the_Vivrti_on_the_end_of_Chapter_1_8](https://www.academia.edu/32230777/In_search_of_Utpaladeva_s_lost_Vivrti_on_the_Pratyabhijna_treatise_a_report_on_the_latest_discoveries_with_the_Vivrti_on_the_end_of_Chapter_1_8)

24. **Torella — "Some Hitherto Unknown Fragments of Utpaladeva's Vivṛti"**
    Reconstructs central arguments otherwise known mainly through Abhinavagupta.
    [https://www.academia.edu/70706930/A_fragment_of_Utpaladevas_%C4%AA%C5%9Bvarapratyabhij%C3%B1%C4%81_viv%E1%B9%9Bti](https://www.academia.edu/70706930/A_fragment_of_Utpaladevas_%C4%AA%C5%9Bvarapratyabhij%C3%B1%C4%81_viv%E1%B9%9Bti)

25. **Torella — "The Importance of Utpaladeva"**
    A concise explanation of why Utpaladeva, not only Abhinavagupta, is the philosophical centre of Pratyabhijñā.
    [https://www.academia.edu/25065673/Importance_of_Utpaladeva](https://www.academia.edu/25065673/Importance_of_Utpaladeva)

26. **Marco Ferrante — "Indian Perspectives on Consciousness, Language and Self"** ★
    Argues that Pratyabhijñā consciousness is intrinsically linguistic and self-aware. Compare with higher-order thought, predictive categorization and theories of language-shaped cognition.
    [https://www.academia.edu/43660090/2021_Indian_Perspectives_on_Consciousness_Language_and_Self_The_School_of_Recognition_on_Linguistics_and_Philosophy_of_Mind_Routledge_Hindu_Studies_Series](https://www.academia.edu/43660090/2021_Indian_Perspectives_on_Consciousness_Language_and_Self_The_School_of_Recognition_on_Linguistics_and_Philosophy_of_Mind_Routledge_Hindu_Studies_Series)

27. **Joshua Stoll — "Inference, Perception, and Recognition: Kashmir Śaivism and Other Minds"** ★
    A direct contemporary-philosophy treatment of other minds and self-manifest consciousness.
    [https://www.academia.edu/38629413/Inference_Perception_and_Recognition_Kashmir_Shaivism_and_the_Problem_of_Other_Minds](https://www.academia.edu/38629413/Inference_Perception_and_Recognition_Kashmir_Shaivism_and_the_Problem_of_Other_Minds)

28. **"Causal Reasoning in the Trika Philosophy of Abhinavagupta"**
    Contrasts Śaiva manifestation with Buddhist causal succession and rival Indian theories of causation.
    [https://www.academia.edu/42815915/Causal_Reasoning_in_the_Trika_Philosophy_of_Abhinavagupta](https://www.academia.edu/42815915/Causal_Reasoning_in_the_Trika_Philosophy_of_Abhinavagupta)

29. **Torella and Bettina Bäumer, eds. — *Utpaladeva: Philosopher of Recognition***
    A multi-author research volume rather than one paper, but several chapters are core readings on cognition, language, freedom and recognition.
    [https://www.academia.edu/19727241/R_Torella_B_Baeumer_Eds_Utpaladeva_Philosopher_of_Recognition_DKPrintworld_Delhi_2015](https://www.academia.edu/19727241/R_Torella_B_Baeumer_Eds_Utpaladeva_Philosopher_of_Recognition_DKPrintworld_Delhi_2015)

30. **Dyczkowski — "Self Awareness, Own Being and Egoity"**
    A central distinction between luminous self-awareness, actual being and constructed egoity. This can be placed beside minimal-self and narrative-self models.
    [https://archive.org/details/self-awareness-own-being-and-egoity](https://archive.org/details/self-awareness-own-being-and-egoity)

# II. Trika–Buddhist confrontation

31. **Ratié — "A Śaiva Interpretation of the Satkāryavāda"**
    Useful for contrasting manifestation with Buddhist production and disappearance.
    [https://college-de-france.academia.edu/IsabelleRati%C3%A9](https://college-de-france.academia.edu/IsabelleRati%C3%A9)

32. **Ratié — "The Buddhist Critique of the Self and the Pratyabhijñā Response"**
    The core dispute is whether continuity, recognition and memory require a real subject or merely a causally connected stream.
    [https://college-de-france.academia.edu/IsabelleRati%C3%A9](https://college-de-france.academia.edu/IsabelleRati%C3%A9)

33. **Torella — "Studies in Utpaladeva's Īśvarapratyabhijñā-vivṛti"**
    Makes visible the extraordinary degree to which Pratyabhijñā was constructed through direct engagement with Buddhist epistemology.
    [https://uniroma1.academia.edu/RaffaeleTorella](https://uniroma1.academia.edu/RaffaeleTorella)

34. **Sonam Kachru — "Ratnakīrti and the Extent of Inner Space"** ★
    Explores Yogācāra and the threat of genuine solipsism. Pair with Ratié on other conscious streams and Trika's single consciousness appearing as many subjects.
    [https://www.academia.edu/39044953/Ratnak%C4%ABrti_and_the_Extent_of_Inner_Space_an_Essay_on_Yog%C4%81c%C4%81ra_and_the_Threat_of_Genuine_Solipsism](https://www.academia.edu/39044953/Ratnak%C4%ABrti_and_the_Extent_of_Inner_Space_an_Essay_on_Yog%C4%81c%C4%81ra_and_the_Threat_of_Genuine_Solipsism)

35. **Kachru — "The Curious Case of the Conscious Corpse"**
    A Buddhist body-replacement thought experiment about personal identity, embodiment and continuity. Ideal for comparison with body-schema plasticity.
    [https://www.academia.edu/45108237/The_Curious_Case_of_the_Conscious_Corpse_A_Medieval_Buddhist_Thought_Experiment](https://www.academia.edu/45108237/The_Curious_Case_of_the_Conscious_Corpse_A_Medieval_Buddhist_Thought_Experiment)

36. **Kachru — "What Is It Like to Become a Likeness of Oneself?"**
    Examines image, motion, self-representation and transformed subjectivity. Relevant to tantric deity identification.
    [https://www.academia.edu/11986098/What_is_it_Like_to_Become_a_Likeness_of_Oneself](https://www.academia.edu/11986098/What_is_it_Like_to_Become_a_Likeness_of_Oneself)

37. **Christian Coseru — "Presence of Mind: Consciousness and the Sense of Self"** ★
    Asks whether Buddhist reductionism can preserve the irreducible first-personal presence of awareness. Direct bridge to prakāśa-vimarśa.
    [https://www.academia.edu/87952909/Presence_of_Mind_Consciousness_and_the_Sense_of_Self](https://www.academia.edu/87952909/Presence_of_Mind_Consciousness_and_the_Sense_of_Self)

38. **Matthew MacKenzie — "Luminosity, Subjectivity, and Temporality"** ★
    Compares Buddhist and Advaita accounts of luminous consciousness, subjectivity and temporal flow. Substitute Trika into the comparison and you have a ready-made research programme.
    [https://www.academia.edu/36387414/Luminosity_Subjectivity_and_Temporality_An_Examination_of_Buddhist_and_Advaita_Views_of_Consciousness](https://www.academia.edu/36387414/Luminosity_Subjectivity_and_Temporality_An_Examination_of_Buddhist_and_Advaita_Views_of_Consciousness)

39. **MacKenzie — "Enacting the Self"** ★
    Treats selfhood as embodied, embedded and self-organizing rather than either a substance or fiction. A powerful mediator between Buddhist anātman and Śaiva agency.
    [https://www.academia.edu/245595/Enacting_the_self_Buddhist_and_Enactivist_approaches_to_the_emergence_of_the_self](https://www.academia.edu/245595/Enacting_the_self_Buddhist_and_Enactivist_approaches_to_the_emergence_of_the_self)

40. **MacKenzie — "Reflexivity, Subjectivity, and the Constructed Self"**
    Distinguishes minimal reflexive awareness from socially and conceptually constructed identity.
    [https://colostate.academia.edu/MatthewMacKenzie](https://colostate.academia.edu/MatthewMacKenzie)

41. **MacKenzie — "The Illumination of Consciousness"**
    Useful for comparing Buddhist svasaṃvedana with self-luminosity theories outside Buddhism.
    [https://colostate.academia.edu/MatthewMacKenzie](https://colostate.academia.edu/MatthewMacKenzie)

42. **Monima Chadha — "Self-Awareness: Eliminating the Myth of the Subject"**
    Develops a Buddhist account in which cognition knows itself without an enduring observer.
    [https://monash.academia.edu/MonimaChadha](https://monash.academia.edu/MonimaChadha)

43. **Chadha — "Perceptual Cognition: A Nyāya–Buddhist Controversy"**
    Useful for the dispute over whether perception presents external objects, internal forms or enacted affordances.
    [https://monash.academia.edu/MonimaChadha](https://monash.academia.edu/MonimaChadha)

44. **Dan Arnold — "Is Svasaṃvitti Transcendental?"**
    Tests whether Buddhist reflexive awareness secretly requires something like a transcendental subject.
    [https://chicago.academia.edu/DanArnold](https://chicago.academia.edu/DanArnold)

45. **Dan Arnold — "Buddhist Idealism, Epistemic and Otherwise"**
    Clarifies the difference between epistemological representationalism and metaphysical idealism. Essential before calling either Yogācāra or Trika "idealism."
    [https://chicago.academia.edu/DanArnold](https://chicago.academia.edu/DanArnold)

46. **John Taber — "The Meaning of Kumārila's Svataḥprāmāṇya"**
    Not Trika, but vital background for intrinsic awareness and self-validating cognition—positions transformed by Utpaladeva.
    [https://unm.academia.edu/JohnTaber](https://unm.academia.edu/JohnTaber)

47. **Birgit Kellner — "Self-Awareness and Infinite Regress"**
    Reconstructs Buddhist arguments that awareness must know itself intrinsically to avoid another cognition ad infinitum.
    [https://oeaw.academia.edu/BirgitKellner](https://oeaw.academia.edu/BirgitKellner)

48. **Kellner — "First-Person Access in Buddhist Epistemology"**
    Useful for separating epistemic access, phenomenal reflexivity and metaphysical selfhood.
    [https://oeaw.academia.edu/BirgitKellner](https://oeaw.academia.edu/BirgitKellner)

49. **Georges Dreyfus — "Self-Awareness: A Case Study in Buddhist Philosophy of Mind"**
    A clean entry into the Tibetan reception of Indian reflexive-awareness debates.
    [https://williams.academia.edu/GeorgesDreyfus](https://williams.academia.edu/GeorgesDreyfus)

50. **Chiara Mascarello — *Self-Awareness in Tibetan Buddhism***
    A recent open-access monograph connecting rang rig to contemporary consciousness debates.
    [https://doi.org/10.30687/978-88-6969-929-0](https://doi.org/10.30687/978-88-6969-929-0)

# III. Early Buddhism and cognitive science

51. **Anālayo — "Mindfulness and the Construction of Experience"** ★
    His most important account of consciousness, perception, attention, intention and concept formation.
    [https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/5-personen/analayo/intheseen.pdf](https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/5-personen/analayo/intheseen.pdf)

52. **Anālayo — "Consciousness and Dependent Arising"** ★
    Explains continuity without a permanent subject through reciprocal dependence between consciousness and name-and-form.
    [https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/5-personen/analayo/consciousnessdependentarising.pdf](https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/5-personen/analayo/consciousnessdependentarising.pdf)

53. **Anālayo — "Attention and Mindfulness"**
    Distinguishes ever-present selection from deliberately cultivated receptive monitoring.
    [https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/5-personen/analayo/attentionmindfulness.pdf](https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/5-personen/analayo/attentionmindfulness.pdf)

54. **Anālayo — "On Time"**
    Identifies the explanatory difficulties created by radical momentariness, especially memory and continuity.
    [https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/5-personen/analayo/time.pdf](https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/5-personen/analayo/time.pdf)

55. **Anālayo — "Acitta in Early Prajñāpāramitā"** ★
    Asks whether "without mind" means unconsciousness, cessation or awareness released from signs.
    [https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/5-personen/analayo/acitta.pdf](https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/5-personen/analayo/acitta.pdf)

56. **Coseru — "From the Five Aggregates to Phenomenal Consciousness"** ★
    Uses the aggregates to formulate hypotheses concerning attention, memory and phenomenal awareness.
    [https://www.academia.edu/2011639/From_the_Five_Aggregates_to_Phenomenal_Consciousness_Toward_a_Cross_Cultural_Cognitive_Science](https://www.academia.edu/2011639/From_the_Five_Aggregates_to_Phenomenal_Consciousness_Toward_a_Cross_Cultural_Cognitive_Science)

57. **Coseru — "Précis of Perceiving Reality"**
    Presents Buddhist epistemology as a phenomenologically informed, naturalized theory of cognition.
    [https://cofc.academia.edu/ChristianCoseru](https://cofc.academia.edu/ChristianCoseru)

58. **Coseru — "Consciousness, Physicalism, and Mental Causation"** ★

# IV. Predictive processing, meditation and nondual awareness

71. **Ruben Laukkonen and Heleen Slagter — "From Many to (N)One"** ★
    Proposes meditation as progressively reducing predictive and conceptual processing. This is one of the best contemporary mechanisms to compare with nirvikalpa and recognition.
    [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.06.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.06.021)

72. **Laukkonen et al. — "Catching the Present Moment"**
    Uses predictive processing to explain how meditation may loosen temporally deep models and increase sensory immediacy.
    [https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/3p4ke](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/3p4ke)

73. **Laukkonen and Slagter — "The Elephant and the Blind"**
    Connects insight, meditation and altered model structure while warning against single-level explanations.
    [https://rubenlaukkonen.com/publications/](https://rubenlaukkonen.com/publications/)

74. **Kathryn Dorjee — "Defining Contemplative Science"**
    Distinguishes contemplative constructs carefully enough to avoid treating all meditation as one intervention.
    [https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01788](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01788)

75. **John Dunne — "Toward an Understanding of Non-dual Mindfulness"** ★
    Distinguishes constructive attention practices from styles that seek awareness without subject–object structuring.

76. **Dunne, Lutz and Davidson — "Meditation and the Neuroscience of Consciousness"**

77. **Josipovic — "Nondual Awareness: Consciousness-as-Such as Non-representational Reflexivity"** ★

# V. Empirical contemplative neuroscience

87. **Lutz et al. — "Long-Term Meditators Self-Induce High-Amplitude Gamma Synchrony"**
    Classic evidence that intensive practice alters large-scale neural coordination. It does not prove "higher consciousness," but constrains claims about trained integration.
    [https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0407401101](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0407401101)

88. **Lutz et al. — "Attention Regulation and Monitoring in Meditation"**
    Introduces the focused-attention/open-monitoring distinction that still organizes much meditation science.
    [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2008.01.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2008.01.005)

89. **Lutz et al. — "Guiding the Study of Brain Dynamics Using First-Person Data"**
    Demonstrates how phenomenological reports can sort neural trials more meaningfully.
    [https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.032658199](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.032658199)

90. **Farb et al. — "Attending to the Present: Distinct Neural Modes of Self-Reference"**
    Contrasts narrative self-processing with present-centered experiential processing.
    [https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsm030](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsm030)

91. **Brewer et al. — "Meditation Experience Is Associated with Differences in Default Mode Network Activity"**
    Influential evidence relating practice to reduced activity and altered coupling in self-referential networks.
    [https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1112029108](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1112029108)

92. **Hasenkamp et al. — "Mind Wandering and Attention During Focused Meditation"**
    Breaks meditation into a cycle: wandering, awareness of wandering, shifting attention and sustained focus.
    [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2011.07.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2011.07.008)

93. **Tang, Hölzel and Posner — "The Neuroscience of Mindfulness Meditation"**
    Authoritative overview of attention, emotion regulation, self-processing and plasticity.
    [https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn3916](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn3916)

94. **Fox et al. — "Is Meditation Associated with Altered Brain Structure?"**
    Meta-analysis separating recurrent structural findings from inflated individual-study claims.
    [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2014.03.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2014.03.016)

95. **Fox et al. — "Functional Neuroanatomy of Meditation"**
    Meta-analysis showing that different meditation families recruit distinguishable networks.
    [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2016.03.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2016.03.021)

96. **Britton et al. — "Defining and Measuring Meditation-Related Adverse Effects"**
    Important methodological control: altered states and difficult experiences must not automatically be redescribed as spiritual progress.
    [https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0176239](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0176239)

97. **Van Dam et al. — "Mind the Hype: A Critical Evaluation of Mindfulness Research"** ★
    Essential scientific hygiene concerning weak constructs, expectancy effects, poor controls and exaggerated conclusions.
    [https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691617709589](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691617709589)

98. **Berkovich-Ohana et al. — "Default Mode Network Connectivity and Meditation"**
    Relevant to self-boundary attenuation, internal mentation and nondual reports.
    [https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nst087](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nst087)

99. **Dor-Ziderman et al. — "Self-Specific Processing in the Meditating Brain"**
    Separates narrative, minimal and "selfless" processing using phenomenology and neural measurement.
    [https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.22181](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.22181)

100. **Millière, Carhart-Harris, Roseman, Trautwein and Berkovich-Ohana — "Psychedelics, Meditation, and Self-Consciousness"** ★
     A strong conceptual comparison of self-loss across meditation and altered states, distinguishing bodily, perspectival and narrative dimensions.
     [https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01475](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01475)

# The deepest initial essay sequence

1. **Is consciousness intrinsically reflexive?**
   Abhinavagupta, Utpaladeva, Dharmakīrti, Coseru, Zahavi and higher-order theories.

2. **Does perception disclose reality or enact a world?**
   Pratyabhijñā idealism, Sautrāntika representationalism, enactivism and predictive processing.

3. **What preserves a person through time?**
   Utpaladeva on memory, Buddhist momentariness, episodic memory and predictive self-models.

4. **Is imagination merely representation or controlled manifestation?**
   Bhāvanā, deity yoga, counterfactual generation and active inference.

5. **What remains when conceptual cognition stops?**
   Nirvikalpa, acitta, cessation, minimal phenomenal experience and nondual-awareness research.

Those five are strong enough to become the theoretical spine of the entire Tantrāloka–consciousness-science project.
